# How to Use This Package in NotebookLM

## What to upload

Upload these four documents as sources in one NotebookLM notebook:

1. `01_People_to_Know_Chapter1_COMPLETED` — the filled-out assignment (18 people, three layers of depth each)
2. `02_Chapter1_Master_Study_Guide` — the complete chapter walkthrough (all 10 learning objectives, every study, every method)
3. `03_Key_Terms_Glossary_and_Self_Test` — every key term defined + the book's own practice questions with official answers + spoken quiz prompts
4. `05_Perspective_Worksheet_COMPLETED` — the filled-out "Chapter 1 – Theories" perspectives worksheet (all 10 perspectives with focus + important people)

(PDF, Word, and Markdown versions are included. For documents 01–03 the content is identical across formats — upload whichever NotebookLM takes. For document 05, upload the **Markdown** version: it carries the full explanations, while the Word/PDF versions are the filled-in worksheet layout for submitting or copying by hand.)

## Suggested podcast customization prompt

When you generate the Audio Overview, paste something like this into the "customize" box:

> Act as if you are tutoring a student through Chapter 1 of an intro psychology course ("Introduction to the Science of Psychology") the night before they fill out two assignments — a "People to Know" worksheet and a "Perspectives/Theories" worksheet — and take a quiz. Go person by person through all 18 people in the People to Know document — Plato, Aristotle, Descartes, Locke, Wundt, Hall, James, Titchener, Darwin, Calkins, Washburn, Clark, Freud, Pavlov, Watson, Skinner, Rogers, and Maslow — giving each one's dates, what they did, which school or perspective they belong to, and the memory hooks. Emphasize the five classic confusions: Wundt vs. Titchener, structuralism vs. functionalism, Calkins vs. Washburn, Pavlov vs. Skinner (classical vs. operant), and the nature-vs-nurture teams. Then walk through the whole chapter in order: the definition and four goals of psychology, the Sam and Anaïs twin story, the eight perspectives from Table 1.1, the five steps of the scientific method, samples, the three research methods and their advantages/disadvantages (Table 1.2), correlation vs. causation with the third-variable and directionality problems, the experimental method vocabulary (IV, DV, extraneous, confounding, placebo, double-blind, experimenter bias, Clever Hans, the SpongeBob study), research ethics (informed consent, debriefing, IRB, confidentiality), and positive psychology. Also walk through the ten perspectives on the Perspectives worksheet — Biological/Neuroscience, Developmental, Cognitive, Psychodynamic, Humanistic, Behavioral, Social-cultural, Evolutionary, Psychoanalytic, and Biopsychosocial — stating each one's focus, how it explains thoughts and behaviors, and its important people, including the difference between psychoanalytic and psychodynamic. Quiz the listener occasionally using the questions in the self-test document, pausing to explain the right answers. Be extremely detailed — more detailed than the textbook — and don't skip anyone or any section.

## Tips

- Ask for a **longer** Audio Overview if the app offers a length option — this is a full chapter plus an 18-person assignment.
- After the podcast, use NotebookLM's chat to quiz yourself: "Ask me the 27 spoken-review questions one at a time and check my answers."
- The two people marked [supplemental] in the documents (G. Stanley Hall and John Locke) are on your assignment list but not in the scanned textbook pages — their entries come from standard psychology history, so double-check them against whatever your teacher said in class.
- When you fill out the physical worksheet, the **"Worksheet answer"** paragraph under each person is written to be exactly the level of detail a fill-in assignment wants — the rest is depth for the test.
